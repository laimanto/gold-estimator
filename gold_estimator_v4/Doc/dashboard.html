⚖ Fair Value — where fundamentals put gold

A second model, separate from the estimator above. The estimator explains moves in gold from moves in the factors; this one fits gold's price level to the factor levels (De-dollar Era, 246 weeks, 12 of the estimator's 14 factors). Fair value = the equation at today's factor values; fair value at 3m forecasts = the same equation at the analyst forecasts in the table above (it updates live when you edit them). The gold price is not an input to either, so neither moves when gold moves — only when a factor does.

Factors deliberately left out of the level model

  • ETF Flow: ETF holdings follow the gold price (buying arrives the quarter after a rise, lead-lag +0.44); the level of holdings is a record of past price moves. Removed from the estimator as well on 2026-11-15: lagged one quarter the flow explains nothing, and this quarter's flow is explained by last quarter's gold move.
  • Jewellery Demand: In levels, jewellery is the demand curve, not a driver: high price → low jewellery (corr −0.89). Including it would read today's weak jewellery as 'gold deserves to be high'. Stays in the change model (demand shocks).
  • Money Supply (M2): The M2 stock is a near-straight line in this era (corr 0.72 with time, 0.67 with CB holdings); its level coefficient flips sign between halves and worsens out-of-sample tracking. M2 growth remains a driver in the change model.

Caveats

  • Central-bank holdings are the slowest input (WGC, ~5 weeks after quarter end). Since the last quarter end the series is carried forward at the last quarter's pace: 173 t assumed, worth +2.7% of today's fair value. Within a quarter the weekly path is interpolated.
  • Central-bank buying also reacts to price (they buy less after rallies), so that coefficient carries some feedback. A static level regression cannot remove it; dynamic OLS was tried and overfits at twelve factors (out-of-sample tracking 7% → 27%), so plain OLS is used.
  • Short history: ~18 independent quarters for 12 coefficients. They will move as data arrives.
  • Shared trend: gold, holdings and several factors all rose over the era; the level R² is partly that trend. The ex-trend weights, the 13-week-change R² and the out-of-sample band are the honest guides.
  • Change-model residual (previous fair-value method): over the last 3 months gold moved -13.9% versus what the estimator's factors explain (z=-2.67); residual-implied level $4,513. Kept as a diagnostic only.

Why this is not a model factor. Managed-money net length is gold buying, so putting it on the right-hand side of the regression would be partly tautological — "gold rose because funds bought gold" — in a way that TIPS or DXY are not. Screening (2026-08-06) also found no predictive content: correlation with the next week's gold return is −0.14, and at a true non-overlapping 13-week lag it is statistically insignificant (p=0.23). What it does tell you is how much squeeze fuel is loaded: a washed-out reading means a break of technical resistance can force short covering, which is what produced the +4.5% single day on 2026-08-05. Read it as a risk gauge on the model's forecast, not as an input to it.

📅 Data Release Schedule — when each input actually publishes

Two FRED inputs land a full business day late, at different hours: GVZ posts ~09:35 ET on T+1 and H.15 (TIPS / credit spread / fed funds) ~16:15 ET on T+1. The Friday W-FRI bar the 13-week model regresses on is therefore not complete until Monday ~16:30 ET, when Friday's H.15 lands. Anything earlier sees complete data only through T-2 and silently forward-fills the rest. The pipeline runs once per ISO week, triggered at logon, and holds off until that Friday has actually published rather than trusting a clock — so an early-morning boot defers to the next one. Research cadence is release-driven, not calendar-driven: each weekly run compares every series' latest observation against the one recorded at the previous run, and only re-researches a tier when new data has actually landed. Running before a publication therefore costs at most one week, never a month. 🧮 Factor Methodology

Factor In-Model Form Purpose
Gold (dependent)Δln(Gold_Nominal)Frequency: Daily. Source: Yahoo Finance: GC=F.
Monthly % return in nominal gold price. No CPI deflation needed — inflation is captured through TIPS (nominal rate = TIPS + breakeven inflation), so deflating would be redundant. Δln(Gold) gives a stationary proportional return series suitable for OLS.
Rates & Inflation
Real Rate (TIPS)Δ percentage pointsFrequency: Daily. Source: FRED: DFII10.
Monthly change in the 10yr TIPS yield = the new information that triggers reallocation. Breakeven (inflation expectation) is shown as a decomposition only — it is collinear with TIPS since nominal rate = TIPS + breakeven, so including both would introduce perfect multicollinearity. TIPS directly captures the combined real-rate effect.
US Dollar & Money Supply
US Dollar (DXY)Δ% log-returnFrequency: Daily. Source: Yahoo Finance: DX-Y.NYB.
Log-diff captures proportional impact: 1% dollar move at DXY=90 and DXY=110 both represent the same purchasing-power shift for global gold buyers.
Money Supply (M2)Level % YoYFrequency: Monthly (~4-week lag after month-end; quarterly revision). Source: FRED: M2SL.
An ongoing M2 growth rate of 5% creates a persistent tailwind each month — differencing would capture only acceleration/deceleration, missing the continuous effect.
Risk & Uncertainty
Market Fear (VIX)Δ% log-returnFrequency: Daily (real-time during US market hours). Source: Yahoo Finance: ^VIX.
Proportional VIX moves drive equivalent safe-haven flows. VIX is log-normal (Whaley 2009).
Bond Volatility (MOVE)Δ% log-returnFrequency: Daily. Source: Yahoo Finance: ^MOVE (ICE BofA MOVE index).
Implied volatility of US Treasury yields from 1-month options on 2/5/10/30yr Treasuries — the bond-market analogue of VIX. Captures rate uncertainty beyond the rate direction already in TIPS: with the real-rate move controlled, higher rate volatility drove hedging demand into gold during the QE era (taper tantrum, liftoff episodes). QE Era factor only: excluded from the De-dollar Era model — its effect there is statistically indistinguishable from zero (p ≈ 0.4) and adds under 0.3% explanatory power, versus a stable, significant contribution in the QE Era. Structural demand (CB buying, de-dollarization) displaced Fed-policy uncertainty as gold's dominant driver after Mar 2022. Added in v3 (screened 2026-07; stable in both QE-era halves, p<0.01 ex-COVID; low overlap with VIX: corr ≈ 0.5, VIF 1.7).
Gold Option Vol (GVZ)Δ% log-returnFrequency: Daily. Source: FRED: GVZCLS (CBOE Gold ETF Volatility Index).
30-day implied volatility priced into GLD options — the gold market's own VIX, read straight off the option chain rather than inferred from realised prices. Captures what VIX cannot: demand for gold optionality (tail hedging, call-skew chasing) as distinct from generic equity risk-off. De-dollar Era factor only. The sign is positive — since Mar 2022 gold rallies have been volatility-expanding melt-ups, the inverse of the classic 'vol up → commodities down' pattern. Added in v4 (screened 2026-08-06: 13w HAC p<0.0001, weight 24%, VIF 2.2, adj R² 0.742 → 0.842). Caveat — read this before trusting the weight: GVZ is a coincident measure. It carries essentially no predictive content (corr with next-week gold return ≈ 0.14, and it flips sign at a true non-overlapping 13-week lag), so it sharpens the model's explanation of a completed quarter, not its forecast of the next one. In forecast mode your GVZ input is itself a guess about gold's own turbulence, and the +24% weight will amplify that guess. It also absorbs VIX (weight 1.1% → 0.3%, p=0.35) and Credit Spread (→ 0.0%, p=0.83): in the De-dollar Era, gold responds to gold-specific fear, not generic risk-off. Excluded from the QE Era, where its effect is indistinguishable from zero (p=0.94) — that era asymmetry is the evidence it is not merely restating gold's own realised move.
Policy Uncertainty (EPU)Δ% log-returnFrequency: Monthly (~1-month lag; academic index, no live market ticker). Source: FRED: USEPUINDXM (Baker, Bloom & Davis).
Log-diff normalises extreme spikes (Baker/Bloom/Davis 2016 use log-EPU in their regressions).
Credit / Default RiskΔ percentage pointsFrequency: Daily. Source: FRED: BAA10Y (Moody's Baa minus 10yr Treasury).
A +0.5pp widening signals the same incremental default stress regardless of the absolute spread level.
Financial Markets
Stock Market (QQQ)Δ% log-returnFrequency: Daily. Source: Yahoo Finance: QQQ.
Monthly % return — risk-off rotation into gold responds to the magnitude of equity loss, not the index level. QQQ used for higher beta exposure vs S&P 500.
Commodity Index (ex-Gold)Δ% log-returnFrequency: Daily. Source: Yahoo Finance: DJP (iPath Bloomberg Commodity ETF).
DJP log-return mathematically stripped of gold's ~12.7% index weight: (Δln_DJP − 0.127 × Δln_Gold) / 0.873. Removes the circularity where BCOM partially explains gold because gold IS in BCOM. The residual captures commodity supercycle dynamics — energy, industrial metals, agriculture — that independently co-move with gold through inflation expectations and risk-on/off channels. Partial R²: 3.7%** (QE Era) / 12.0%*** (De-dollar Era).
Oil (Commodity-Adj.)Δ% residual log-returnFrequency: Daily. Source: Yahoo Finance: CL=F (WTI front-month futures).
Stage 1b orthogonalization: D_ln_Oil regressed on TIPS, DXY, and M2 to strip the inflation-proxy component already captured by TIPS. Oil_Residual = actual Δln(Oil) − macro-predicted = pure localized commodity dynamics (OPEC+ quota shifts, refinery bottlenecks, strategic reserve releases). Negligible residual weight (~0.6%) confirms oil's systemic signal is fully absorbed by TIPS.
Structural Demand
CB Net Purchasestonnes/quarter (t/qtr)Frequency: Quarterly (~6–8 weeks after quarter end: Q1→May, Q2→Aug, Q3→Nov, Q4→Feb). Source: WGC: Gold Demand Trends (GDT_Tables_Q?xx_EN.xlsx, Gold Balance sheet — free registration required).
Strategic sovereign reserve accumulation (de-dollarization, sanction-proofing). Price-inelastic and independent of short-term macro conditions. No differencing needed — level directly measures ongoing accumulation pace.
ETF Flow (WGC) — conditional inputtonnes/quarter (raw total flow)Source: WGC: Gold Demand Trends (quarterly, ~5 weeks after quarter end).
Global gold-ETF net flow, entered as the quarterly rate. Read it as a conditional input: ETF investors mostly follow gold — with GLD's daily share count, last week's gold move predicts this week's ETF buying (t ≈ +4 to +6) while ETF buying does not predict next week's gold move. Its coefficient therefore mixes ‘ETF demand lifts gold’ with ‘rising gold attracts ETF demand’, and a 3-month ETF-flow forecast partly embeds a view on gold itself. Given that forecast it still improves the pooled model's walk-forward error from 6.7% to 5.6% (about +2.6% on gold per +100 t/qtr). Uses the raw flow (the v3–v4 macro-adjusted residual is retired, so the forecast you enter is the same quantity the model was fitted on). Excluded from the fair-value level model: the stock of holdings is a record of past buying and worsened out-of-sample tracking.

WGC quarterly retail investment demand (physical bullion, sovereign coins). Proxies for retail safe-haven sentiment and store-of-value buying, especially in Asia and the Middle East. Interpolated from quarterly to weekly.

Factors tested but not included:

  • Geopolitical Risk Index (GPR) (Caldara & Iacoviello; daily, 1985–present; policyuncertainty.com/gpr.html) — carries the correct positive sign but adds no incremental explanatory power once TIPS, DXY, and ETF sentiment are controlled for (partial R² <1% in both eras, n.s.). Excluded: parsimony, no incremental R².
  • Bitcoin (BTC-USD log-return) (daily; Yahoo Finance: BTC-USD) — tested as a digital-gold substitution proxy; marginally negative sign in QE Era, significance disappears entirely in De-dollar Era where TIPS and ETF already absorb speculative positioning. Excluded: parsimony, out-of-sample instability.
  • USD/CNY & USD/INR (daily; Yahoo: CNY=X / INR=X) — tested 2026-07 as China / India demand channels (neither currency is in the DXY basket). Looked significant over the full De-dollar Era (CNY p=0.08, INR p=0.02) but failed half-sample verification: strongly negative 2022–24, sign flips 2024–26. CNY also absorbs TIPS's significance (p 0.009 → 0.19) rather than adding information. Excluded: out-of-sample instability, factor cannibalization.
  • Breakeven Inflation (T10YIE), Fed Funds (DFF), Yield-Curve Slope (T10Y2Y) (FRED, daily) — all n.s. in both eras once TIPS, DXY and M2 are controlled (tested 2026-07). Breakeven remains available as the TIPS decompose input; it carries no incremental power as a standalone factor.
  • 10yr Term Premium (ACM, FRED THREEFYTP10) — the fiscal-risk narrative factor; n.s. in both eras and heavily collinear with TIPS (VIF ≈ 11 in the De-dollar Era). TIPS already carries the term-premium signal.
  • Chicago Fed NFCI & High-Yield OAS (BAMLH0A0HYM2) (FRED, weekly/daily) — financial-conditions gauges; overlap Credit Spread too strongly (corr ≈ 0.77 / VIF ≈ 15). NFCI marginal in QE Era only (p=0.07); HY OAS history too short (2023+). Excluded: redundant with Credit Spread.

v5 changes (Nov 2026):

  • Fair-value level model added (De-dollar Era only; 12 factors) — see the Fair Value card.
  • ETF flow: briefly removed (v5.1) because it follows the price rather than leading it; reinstated in v5.2 as a conditional input (raw WGC flow) — given a 3-month ETF forecast it improves the walk-forward error 6.7% → 5.6%.
  • Estimator pooled across eras (v5.2). Factor-move relationships are the same in both eras except gold option vol, so one 2010–2026 fit with an era shift and an era-specific GVZ slope replaced the two separate fits (walk-forward error 10.2% → 6.7%). The fair-value model keeps the 2022 cut: since Mar 2022 the sensitivities changed, not the factor list. Each tonne of central-bank buying now moves the fair value about twice as much (+1.55% vs +0.78% per 100 t — price-insensitive, sanctions-driven buyers), real rates matter about half as much (−7% vs −16% per pp), and stocks and gold now rise together. With post-2022 sensitivities the factors explain +65 of gold's +68 log-point rise; with pre-2022 sensitivities they predict −40. The exchange-rate dollar (DXY) rose over the period, so the de-dollarisation effect is carried by central-bank buying, not by DXY. US debt/GDP, foreign Treasury holdings, the Fed balance sheet, the broad dollar, CPI and geopolitical risk were tested and add nothing.
  • MOVE dropped with the pooling (QE-only factor; adds 0.2 pp of accuracy).

v3 changes (Jul 2026):

  • MOVE index added as a QE-Era factor (see Risk & Uncertainty methodology above).
  • ETF Stage-1 orthogonalization moved to the 13-week-window level. Total R² is unchanged by construction — the fix redistributes attribution: TIPS reclaims macro weight that previously leaked into the ETF residual (De-dollar Era TIPS β strengthened, p 0.009 → <0.001 at the time of the change).
  • Known residual interdependencies (documented, not orthogonalized): Oil↔Commodity Index r ≈ 0.74 (orthogonalizing oil against BCOM would reallocate the commodity β without changing fit — kept as-is so both βs stay separately interpretable); WGC structural-demand block VIF 7–9 (artifact of quarterly→weekly interpolation; affects attribution precision only, not fit or forecasts).
